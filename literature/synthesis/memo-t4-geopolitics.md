## What is established

1. Geopolitical change alters how multinationals organize, not only where
   they invest. Reviews and perspectives converge on this
   (`moura2026how`, 93 papers per the OpenAlex abstract; `patsiaouras2026geopolitical`,
   138 articles; `sinani2026caught`, with corporate nationality shaping
   organizational design and value-chain configuration). De-globalization
   and decoupling are expected to shift IB structures, with liberal and
   realist accounts predicting different patterns (`witt2019deglobalization`,
   `witt2023decoupling`), and techno-nationalism creates bifurcated
   governance (`petricevic2019structural`).

2. Geopolitical change affects foreign employment. Volatility of bilateral
   political affinity reduces the number of subsidiaries, employees and
   local sales of US firms (`adarkwah2024geopolitical`, 1,054 firms, 106
   countries, 2000-2015).

3. Units within one firm can respond differently to the same geopolitical
   change. Under home-host political tension, IP ownership is centralized at
   the ultimate owner while subsidiaries raise local inventor employment and
   HQs cut reliance on host inventors (`tang2026strategic`, 637,690 patents).
   This is the closest corpus evidence for selective, unit-specific
   reconfiguration, and it includes a staffing outcome (local inventor
   employment), though at the unit level and in AOM Proceedings form.

4. Local partners carry government-facing capability. Partnering with a host
   firm that is good at dealing with government mitigates political
   hazards, at the risk of partner expropriation
   (`henisz2000institutional`). Host-government demands are a long-standing
   source of responsiveness pressure (`doz1981global`, T2 record).

5. Shocks shift the ownership choice. After Entity List designation, Chinese
   MNEs favor wholly owned subsidiaries over JVs (`lei2025playing`), which
   implies survivor and selection issues for any IJV sample observed after a
   shock.

## What is disputed

1. Adapt locally or retreat. `meyer2022mne` expects pressure for local
   adaptation; `moura2026how` distinguishes shaping, adaptive engagement,
   non-engagement and exit; `tang2026strategic` shows both at once in
   different units. Whether a given shock leads to localization inside the
   unit or to withdrawal from it is open, which is why H4 is framed as a
   within-IJV shift conditional on survival.

2. What theory predicts. `witt2019deglobalization` contrasts liberal and
   realist predictions; `meyer2022mne` adds constructivism. None of these
   predicts domain-level differences; that is the role of the contingency
   argument.

## What is measured and how

Bilateral affinity from UN voting and its volatility
(`adarkwah2024geopolitical`); designation events with matched
difference-in-differences (`lei2025playing`); patent filing and inventor
location (`tang2026strategic`); entry-mode choice under political hazard
indices (`henisz2000institutional`). Reviews discuss measures of geopolitics
(`sinani2026caught`). No corpus study measures shock exposure at the IJV
parent-host pair level or the response at the function or occupation level.

## Implication for our theory section

T4 supplies the framing that a geopolitical change is a change in the
contingencies of some domains and not others (`meyer2022mne` names
regulatory divergence and reduced mobility as the relevant disruptions), and
the evidence that firms' units respond differently to the same change
(`tang2026strategic`) and that foreign employment responds to bilateral
politics (`adarkwah2024geopolitical`). For S13 the corpus supports only the
general form of both channels; every Brexit-specific step in the H4
instantiation carries a `[CITE: ...]` marker until the shock-scan
references are verified and the S9 runs are ingested. The paper also has to
frame Brexit as a change in the political order between states (gate 5a,
decision 4); `meyer2022mne` and `witt2019deglobalization` are the corpus
works that can carry that framing.
