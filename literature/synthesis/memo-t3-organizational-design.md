## What is established (from records in other streams)

1. Control mechanisms come in recognizable types that differ in what they
   need from people. Output, process and social control in IJVs
   (`chen2009parent`) and behavior, outcome and clan control
   (`han2019management`) follow a typology whose source is not in the corpus
   `[CITE: Ouchi 1979; Eisenhardt 1985]`. Social or clan control is the type
   most tied to personnel: it works through socialization, which
   `edstrom1977transfer` links to manager transfers and `harzing2001bears`
   to expatriates as "bumble-bees". Direct versus indirect parental control
   is a further distinction (`park2009foreign`).

2. The choice of control mechanism responds to task characteristics. Task
   interdependence raises behavior control in IJVs (`han2019management`),
   and parents adjust control over the life cycle (`lenguyen2009parent`).

3. Hierarchical layers can carry distinct coordination roles. Intermediate
   units between HQ and subsidiaries take coordinative or entrepreneurial
   roles (`plabarber2021intermediate`), and MNE structures (simple HQ,
   network, matrix) open different development paths for subsidiaries
   (`raziq2023multinational`). These are units, not employee levels, but
   they are the only corpus support for the idea that one layer of an
   organization specializes in coordination.

4. Hierarchy can be measured from profile data and trades off against other
   coordination mechanisms. Managerial hierarchy estimated from 42 million
   profiles is negatively associated with culture strength, consistent with
   functional equivalence (`marchetti2026are`); hierarchy steepness can be
   inferred from job titles (`heiss2024structure`); AI-investing firms
   flatten (`babina2023firm`).

## What is disputed

Within the corpus there is no explicit dispute on design. The relevant
debate the paper will face, whether decision rights and control are
substitutes for or complements to staffing as integration mechanisms, is
visible only indirectly (`nohria1994differentiated` on structure versus
shared values; `marchetti2026are` on hierarchy versus culture).

## What is measured and how

Control types: survey scales (`chen2009parent`, `han2019management`).
Hierarchy: counts of managers or title-based steepness from profiles
(`marchetti2026are`, `heiss2024structure`, `babina2023firm`). Decision
rights: not measured in any corpus record. `docs/theory-framework.md` already
records that decision rights and control mechanisms are not directly
observable in the archival data; the corpus offers nothing that changes
that.

## What is missing for this project

1. A source for `coord_role`, the claim that senior levels carry
   alliance-wide coordination responsibility while lower levels are tied to
   local operations `[CITE: hierarchical levels and coordination
   responsibility, e.g., Mintzberg 1979 or an alliance-governance source]`.
   This is open in `docs/decisions.md` (2026-11-23): O\*NET scores
   occupations, not Revelio seniority levels, so the level hypotheses still
   need both a theoretical source and a measure.
2. The coordination versus cooperation distinction in alliances
   `[CITE: Gulati, Wohlgezogen & Zhelyazkov 2012]`, which would let the
   paper separate coordination dependence (the contingency) from incentive
   alignment between parents (closer to the bargaining account).
3. Information-processing design `[CITE: Galbraith 1974]` for the link from
   interdependence to lateral integration mechanisms such as liaison roles,
   which parent-origin staff can fill.
4. A design account of how organizations differentiate structure across
   functions `[CITE: Mintzberg 1979]` and how decision rights are allocated
   `[CITE: Jensen & Meckling 1992]`, to state what staffing does not
   capture.
5. A source on organization design as the allocation of tasks and the
   provision of information and rewards `[CITE: Puranam, Alexy & Reitzig
   2014]`, which would frame staffing as part of the "who does what"
   problem.

## Implication for our theory section

Organizational design enters only where the construct and its limits are
defined: staffing is one integration mechanism among several (control
types, decision rights, hierarchy), and the archival data observe staffing
only. The corpus supports the link from task interdependence to process
control (`han2019management`) and from transfers to social control
(`edstrom1977transfer`, `harzing2001bears`), which is enough to justify
reading parent-origin hires as a coordination mechanism. It does not
support any claim about hierarchical levels and coordination roles; until
the S8 runs are ingested, every level-based statement in the outline carries
a `[CITE: ...]` marker. The PI should treat the S8 gap as blocking for the
level hypotheses and not for the occupation-based ones.
