# Memo T2: IB applications of contingency logic (streams S1 and S2)

Stage 2 (lit-synthesis), literature-synthesizer, 2026-11-24.
Evidence basis: worked from abstracts / metadata only. No full texts. Sources
of abstracts as in `memo-t1-structural-contingency.md`; seeds without an
abstract are characterized from the standard reading and marked "to verify".

Framing rule (`docs/theory-framework.md`): the differentiated network and the
integration-responsiveness (IR) framework are treated here as international
business applications of structural contingency logic, not as separate or
rival theories, and neither anchors a hypothesis. Their role is to show that
international settings add contingencies (host-country embeddedness,
cross-border coordination) and to supply vocabulary.

Corpus: S1 has 20 records (5 seeds, 15 includes), S2 has 14 (5 seeds, 9
includes), per `literature/synthesis/concept-matrix.csv`. Two S2 central
works were not chased at Stage 1 (Venaik et al. 2005, Meyer et al. 2014),
and the S2 v1.1 seeds Stopford and Wells 1972 and Egelhoff 1982 were never
searched.

## What is established

1. Units inside a multinational are differentiated to fit their own
   contingencies. HQ-subsidiary governance (centralization, formalization,
   normative integration) varies systematically with the subsidiary's
   environmental complexity and local resources (`ghoshal1989internal`), and
   differentiated fit relates to performance (`nohria1994differentiated`;
   book-length statement in `nohria1997differentiated`, standard reading).
   Subsidiary roles carry different structural contexts, autonomy and
   value-chain configurations (`birkinshaw1995configurations`) and different
   extents and directions of knowledge flows (`gupta1994organizing`). The
   transnational argument holds that the global-local balance is set unit by
   unit and task by task rather than once for the firm
   (`bartlett1989managing`, standard reading, to verify). This is contingency
   logic with the subsidiary as the subunit.

2. Pressures for global integration and local responsiveness differ below
   the firm level. The IR framework places the balance at the level of the
   business and the function (`prahalad1987multinational`, standard reading,
   to verify), and its revision argues that economic, technological,
   political, customer and competitive pressures act differently on
   businesses and on functions and proposes a way to measure them
   (`venaik2004new`). Host-government demands are a distinct source of
   responsiveness pressure with organizational consequences (`doz1981global`).
   Within one function (HRM), firms pursue integration and responsiveness at
   once in several configurations (`chung2024exploring`), and subsidiary HRM
   practices are shaped by localization and dominance effects
   (`pudelko2007countryoforigin`, T3 record).

3. The local and the integration dimensions respond to different
   contingencies. Resource endowments, local competition and home-host
   distance shape a subsidiary's responsiveness and export orientation but
   barely affect its integration (`meyer2014local`). Contingency links are
   stronger in localized than in globalized subsidiaries (`brock2007global`).
   This is the closest T2 support for the brief's claim that geographic
   origin and organizational origin answer to different contingencies.

4. International settings add an interorganizational element. The MNC has
   been modelled as an internally differentiated network embedded in an
   external network, with internal power following resource configuration
   (`ghoshal1990multinational`), and subsidiaries face "institutional
   duality", that is, pressure from the host institutional environment and
   from the parent at the same time (`kostova2002adoption`, abstract
   truncated, standard reading). These works give vocabulary for a unit
   exposed to two sources of requirements, which the IJV setting doubles on
   the parent side.

5. Coordination is achieved through a portfolio of managerial tools. HQs
   balance responsiveness and coordination with control tools
   (`doz1984patterns`, screening reason only); normative integration and
   communication support innovation in subsidiaries (`ghoshal1988creation`,
   screening reason only); business-unit administrative mechanisms are
   fitted to strategy (`roth1991global`, screening reason only).

## What is disputed

1. One trade-off or two dimensions. The original IR grid is often read as a
   trade-off; `venaik2004new` revisits it and `meyer2014local` and
   `chung2024exploring` find that the dimensions move separately. The paper
   needs the "separate dimensions" reading, so this dispute should be stated
   and resolved in that direction with evidence, not assumed.

2. Unit of analysis. Most S1 work takes the subsidiary as the unit
   (`birkinshaw2009strategy` explicitly defines the field around the wholly
   owned subsidiary), whereas S2 places pressures at business or function
   level (`prahalad1987multinational`, `venaik2004new`, `roth1990empirical`
   at business level). Function-level evidence inside a unit is rare and
   mostly about HRM (`chung2024exploring`, `pudelko2007countryoforigin`).

3. What drives change in unit roles. Subsidiary mandates are gained and lost
   (`birkinshaw1996how`, screening reason), and charters change with
   capabilities (`birkinshaw1998multinational`), driven by initiative,
   resources and autonomy (`birkinshaw1998building`). These dynamics are
   endogenous to the subsidiary; environmental change as the driver appears
   only as an agenda item (`westney2021mncs`). The paper's dynamic claim
   therefore cannot borrow T2 evidence on role change without noting that
   the driver differs.

4. Structural fit versus normative integration. `nohria1994differentiated`
   shows the two as complements. This matters for interpreting staffing:
   parent-origin hires can be read as structural coordination or as carriers
   of shared values.

## What is measured and how

IR pressures and strategies: manager surveys with perceptual scales at
business level (`roth1990empirical`, standard reading) or subsidiary level
(`meyer2014local`, `venaik2005dual`); `venaik2004new` proposes a measurement
methodology for pressures by function (full text needed to see whether it
yields reusable scores). HQ-subsidiary structure: survey scales of
centralization, formalization and normative integration
(`ghoshal1989internal`). Subsidiary roles: typologies from survey data
(`birkinshaw1995configurations`, `gupta1994organizing`). No S1 or S2 work in
the corpus measures integration through staff origin; expatriate presence
appears only in T3. No work uses archival task data to score units.

## Implication for our theory section

T2 enters after the T1 opening as evidence that contingency logic has been
applied inside internationally dispersed organizations, and that
international settings add two contingencies: dependence on the host
environment and need for cross-border coordination (`ghoshal1989internal`,
`prahalad1987multinational`, `venaik2004new`). The strongest T2 point for
this paper is that the local and the integration dimensions respond to
different contingencies (`meyer2014local`), which supports a two-axis
staffing configuration. The interorganizational vocabulary
(`ghoshal1990multinational`, `kostova2002adoption`) can frame the IJV as a
unit facing host and parent requirements, but the extension to two parents
is the paper's own and must not be attributed to T2. No hypothesis is
anchored in T2.
