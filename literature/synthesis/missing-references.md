## 9. Microfoundations flag (for the PI; no memo written)

The literature repeatedly locates the mechanism behind parent-origin
staffing at the individual level:

- Expatriates coordinate through personal informal communication networks
  ("spiders") and socialization ("bumble-bees") (`harzing2001bears`);
  transfers build socialization-based control (`edstrom1977transfer`).
- Host-country CEOs with inpatriation experience gain social ties at
  headquarters, amplified by motivational cultural intelligence
  (`davies2023improving`, upper echelons theory).
- Inpatriates act as individual knowledge agents (`kim2022how`).
- Personality moderates turnover in steep hierarchies
  (`heiss2024structure`), relevant only if retention enters the design.
- The brief's H1 mechanism names "personal ties" carried by the hire
  (`docs/research-brief.md`, H1), and H3's mechanism relies on a hire who
  personally holds both host-specific and parent-specific knowledge.

Assessment: the contingency argument can be stated at the domain level
(the domain's requirement for parent-specific knowledge and coordination),
and the outline does so. But the step from "domain needs parent-specific
knowledge" to "a parent-origin hire supplies it" is carried in the corpus by
individual-level evidence (ties, socialization, cultural intelligence). The
PI should decide whether that step is stated as a domain-level assumption
or whether a narrowly scoped individual-level mechanism is admitted. Per
`docs/theory-framework.md`, nothing has been added.
