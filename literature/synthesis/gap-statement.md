## The three factors

**Gap.** Research on the staffing of foreign units classifies staff mainly
by nationality (parent-country versus host-country nationals) and explains
the mix by control, knowledge transfer and distance (`gong2003subsidiary`,
`gaur2007institutional`, `harzing2001whos`, `harzing2016bridging`,
`inamura2024effect`). Where it adds organizational origin, it does so for the
top position of multinational subsidiaries, as general manager successors
from inside or outside the subsidiary (`li2024origin`), or for categories of
expatriate, such as former inpatriates and self-initiated expatriates
(`georgopoulos2024mediating`). Nationality data alone cannot show whether
foreign staff came from a parent, a limitation that `inamura2024effect`
states for foreign subsidiaries in Japan. Research on IJVs shows that
parental influence is differentiated by activity but measures it as control,
not as hiring sources (`choi2004split`, `mjoen1997control`,
`geringer1989control`, `mohedanosuanes2021control`). Within the bounds of
the search, no study separates the geographic origin of an IJV's wider
workforce from its origin in either parent's group, so localization through
a parent's local workforce cannot be told apart from localization through
the open labor market. Differentiation is documented in a similar partial
way. Across activities it is documented for control, not for hiring sources,
and differences between top management and employees are documented for
nationality-based localization at the level of the unit
(`inamura2024effect`, `harzing2001whos`). Within the bounds of the search, no
study compares hiring sources across functions and levels inside the same
IJV. Nor does the search contain a study of how a change in the environment
shifts staffing across domains inside an IJV; the closest evidence is
unit-level (`tang2026strategic`, `adarkwah2024geopolitical`).

**Importance.** First, the distinction decides what a local staffing share
means: an IJV that localizes through its host-country parent remains
dependent on that parent, whereas one that hires on the open market does
not, and whether localization reduces or re-routes parental dependence is
unresolved in the IJV literature (`inkpen1997knowledge`, `luo2001dual`).
Second, contingency reasoning in IB has been developed mainly for units with
one parent (`ghoshal1989internal`, `nohria1997differentiated`). Where it has
been applied to IJVs, it explains the partners' control systems across IJVs
(`groot2000control`) or the fit of control with the parents' contributions
and with task interdependence (`barden2005influence`, `han2019management`).
Case evidence from Sino-Western IJVs holds that partner power, not
contingencies, decides HRM localization (`wilson2006dynamics`). Within the
bounds of the search, no study asks how two parents and the market, as
sources of staff, are sorted across domains inside an IJV by different
contingencies, which is where an IB contribution to contingency theory can be
made (tension 2 below). Third, geopolitical change is reshaping how
multinationals organize (`meyer2022mne`, `moura2026how`, `sinani2026caught`),
and the reviews call for analysis beyond the home-host dyad
(`patsiaouras2026geopolitical`); practical stakes (for example the number of
IJVs exposed to Brexit or the policy weight host governments give to local
staffing) still need a sourced figure `[CITE: sourced figure on the scale or
policy relevance of IJV staffing or of Brexit-exposed cross-border units]`.

**Method.** Employment histories observe the prior employer and its country
for every hire at every level, which removes the shared limitation directly
(both axes observed, not inferred from nationality), and the occupation
grain is established in profile-based research (`wang2026organizational`,
`liu2020predicting`). Within-IJV comparison across occupation groups with
IJV x year fixed effects holds the IJV-level bargaining outcome constant,
which survey designs with one informant per IJV cannot do
(`yan2001antecedents`, `han2019management`, `liu2014metaanalysis`). The
focal contingency change (S13) has a dated onset and a within-shock
contrast (GB-IE), which permits an event-study design once pre-trends are
checked (`literature/shock-scan/rescoring.md`, section 9).

## Gap type

Primary: **existing studies share a limitation at the level of the
construct and its measurement.** Staffing research measures origin mainly by
nationality, and where it measures organizational origin it does so for one
position or for expatriate categories in units with one parent
(`li2024origin`, `georgopoulos2024mediating`). IJV control research measures
parental influence by activity-level control surveys, typically
cross-sectional with one informant. The two literatures together cannot test
whether localization and dependence on the parents answer to different
contingencies. The fitting design is one that removes the limitation
(multi-level archival panel with prior-employer histories and within-IJV
comparison), which is the design adopted at gate 4.

Secondary, for H3 and H4 only: **the topic has not been studied** within
the bounds stated above (no study of the local parent-origin channel of an
IJV's workforce; no study of within-IJV staffing reconfiguration after a
contingency change). The fitting design is a first empirical test; the claims
must stay associational for H1 to H3 and become causal for H4 only after
pre-trends are checked (`CLAUDE.md`, rule 4).

Rejected as the primary type: "competing theories". Contingency theory and
bargaining power or resource dependence do make different predictions, and
`wilson2006dynamics` states the power-based position for IJV HRM explicitly,
but the design is built to separate them (IJV x year fixed effects), not to
adjudicate between them, and the domain-level variant of bargaining
(`choi2004split`) is not cleanly separated (see tension 3). Treating the gap
as a theory contest would oversell the design.

## The three tensions (`docs/theory-framework.md`)

### 1. Static theory versus dynamic claim

Classical contingency theory specifies fit, not the path to it, and is
read as deterministic. H4 is an adjustment claim: a change in one
contingency produces misfit, and the IJV regains fit by shifting new hiring
on the matching axis only. The adjustment argument rests on
`donaldson1987strategy` (structural adjustment to regain fit) and
`donaldson2001contingency`, and the limit on determinism on
`child1972organizational` (strategic choice). These enter as theory sources,
not as screened corpus records (pre-analysis plan rules, decision 9). Support
from the screened corpus is indirect: parents adjust control over the IJV life
cycle and those that adjust perform better (`lenguyen2009parent`); control
evolves with bargaining power (`yan1994bargaining`); dependence shifts as
partners acquire knowledge (`inkpen1997knowledge`); subsidiary charters
change (`birkinshaw1998multinational`), though by capability, not
environment. How the outline handles the tension: H4 predicts the
direction of change in hiring-flow shares, not a single fitting
configuration, and parents' strategic choice is the reason the prediction is
about shares at the margin (flows), where adjustment is cheapest
`[CITE: hiring flows as the adjustment margin of workforce composition]`.

### 2. Single organization versus two parents

Contingency theory was developed for single organizations, and its IB
applications mainly keep one parent (`ghoshal1989internal`,
`nohria1994differentiated`, `brock2007global`). The interorganizational
element in T2 is a metaphor for the MNC (`ghoshal1990multinational`) or a
duality between host and parent (`kostova2002adoption`). Where contingency
reasoning reaches the IJV, its outcome is control: the partners' control
systems across IJVs (`groot2000control`), the fit of the control structure
with resource contributions (`barden2005influence`) and the link between
task interdependence and control type (`han2019management`). None of these
treats the two parents and the market as sources of staff. The IJV
literature shows the two parents differ in control and its effects
(`luo2001dual`, `chen2009parent`, `barden2005influence`) and that the local
partner holds local and government-facing knowledge (`inkpen1997knowledge`,
`henisz2000institutional`). The IB-specific contribution is that a unit with
two parents and a market has two staffing axes, and the organizational axis
exists only because there is more than one possible organizational source.
H3 is the hypothesis that would not exist for a wholly owned subsidiary, and
its boundary condition (no host-country parent) is the within-design test
of that claim `[CITE: IJVs formed by two foreign parents]`. The
knowledge-combination step of H3 is not itself new: subsidiaries with one
parent that need both intra-firm and local knowledge favor staff who bring
both, such as former inpatriates and self-initiated expatriates
(`georgopoulos2024mediating`; see also `davies2023improving`). What the
paper adds beyond "applying the theory to a new setting" is therefore the
prediction that two contingencies sort hires on two separate axes, with the
parents' host-country workforce as the largest and cheapest channel, though
not the only one, where both contingencies are high, and the within-IJV
domain slope that this channel implies.

### 3. Staffing as an outcome of contingencies and of bargaining power

The IJV literature explains parental control by bargaining power and
resource contribution (`yan2001antecedents`, `yan1994bargaining`,
`liu2014metaanalysis`, `mjoen1997control`, `chen2009parent`,
`killing1983strategies`). Staffing is a control mechanism
(`schaan1983parent`, `harzing2001bears`), so observed parent-origin staffing
may reflect power rather than coordination needs. The rival account has been
stated explicitly for IJV HRM: in four Sino-Western IJVs, partners drew on
resource power, internationalization expertise and consistency requirements
to gain decision power over HRM localization, and the authors conclude that
the contingency approach does not capture the IJV (`wilson2006dynamics`).
The theory section answers this account rather than leaving it unnamed. Its
answer is that partner power and domain contingencies operate at different
grains: power settles who decides at the level of the IJV, whereas the
hypotheses concern differences between domains inside the same IJV-year.
Separability in the design: IJV x year fixed effects absorb IJV-level equity
and bargaining outcomes; domain score x equity share interactions test
whether the contingency slope depends on ownership; the host-country parent
indicator defines the H3 boundary. What is not separated: domain-level
bargaining, in which each parent staffs the activities it contributed
(`choi2004split`, `mohedanosuanes2021control`). This varies within the IJV
and correlates with coordination dependence. The outline therefore proposes
an explicit check (parent industry matched to occupation group) and names
domain-level bargaining as the rival account in the limitations if it
cannot be separated. Ownership asymmetry (equity) and parent dominance
(staffing concentration) are kept as separate variables; the codebook's
equity-adjusted dominance measure is obsolete with the cross-parent
outcomes, so the plan needs a new way to keep them apart `[DATA: equity
shares at hire date; current equity stands in for formation equity per
docs/decisions.md 2026-10-08]`.

Resource dependence theory (open item, `docs/decisions.md` 2026-11-22):
treated here as the rival account, not adopted. Evidence for both readings
is set out in `memo-t3-ijv-control-staffing.md`.

## Contributions as the synthesis phrases them

1. To contingency theory in IB: localization and dependence on the parents
   are separate dimensions of IJV staffing that answer to different
   contingencies (environmental and coordination), so an IJV's staffing
   configuration is differentiated across domains on two axes. This extends
   the differentiation logic of `lawrence1967organization` and its IB
   applications (`ghoshal1989internal`, `meyer2014local`) from units with
   one parent, and from the control systems of IJVs (`groot2000control`), to
   the sources of staff inside a unit with two parents and a market.

2. To IJV staffing and control research: extending the organizational-origin
   dimension from subsidiary general managers and expatriate categories
   (`li2024origin`, `georgopoulos2024mediating`) to the wider workforce of
   IJVs shows a channel of localization through the local parent's
   workforce where coordination dependence is high (H3), which bears on the
   unresolved question of whether localization reduces or re-routes an
   IJV's dependence on its parents (`inkpen1997knowledge`, `luo2001dual`),
   and moves the evidence on activity-level differentiation from control
   surveys (`choi2004split`, `mjoen1997control`) to observed hiring,
   compared across functions and levels inside the same IJV.

3. To research on geopolitics in IB and on contingency dynamics: a change
   in one contingency (regulatory divergence after Brexit) moves staffing
   on the matching axis only, that is, selective reconfiguration inside the
   unit rather than a uniform shift (H4). This complements firm- and
   unit-level evidence on investment, employment and exit
   (`adarkwah2024geopolitical`, `moura2026how`) and on divergent unit
   responses (`tang2026strategic`). Causal language is conditional on the
   event-study pre-trends.

## Weak points a JIBS reviewer will press

- The main theory is backed by one work in the corpus
  (`lawrence1967organization`); see `memo-t1-structural-contingency.md`.
- The contingency scores come from US occupational data and measure generic
  rather than parent-specific coordination (`concept-bridge.md`, section 3).
- The H3 cell is about 0.8 percent of hires (`reviews/gate-4-pilot.md`,
  staffing tabulations, cited from `docs/research-brief.md`), so the
  "not studied" gap may coincide with a rarely occurring phenomenon; the
  importance argument must not rest on H3 alone.
- The knowledge-combination step of H3 has an analog in units with one
  parent (`georgopoulos2024mediating`), so H3 must not be presented as a new
  mechanism; its novelty lies in the channel and the within-IJV slope.
- A two-axis staffing frame (nationality crossed with inside or outside
  origin) already exists for subsidiary general managers (`li2024origin`);
  the frame here must be distinguished from it by unit (the IJV), by
  population (the wider workforce) and by the meaning of organizational
  origin (a parent's group, not the focal unit).
- Domain-level bargaining is the live rival (tension 3), and
  `wilson2006dynamics` gives the power-based reading an explicit IJV
  precedent.
