## Section A. Theoretical framework

**A1. Differentiation and integration across subunits (T1).**
Claim: subunits of one organization face different parts of the environment
and different task interdependencies, so no single arrangement fits the
whole organization; each subunit's structure should match its own
contingencies, and integration across subunits must match the
interdependence among them.
References: `lawrence1967organization`; `thompson1967organizations`;
`galbraith1973designing`; `donaldson2001contingency`.

**A2. The contingency families.** Task, coordination, environmental and
resource contingencies (`docs/theory-framework.md`). The paper uses two
summary scores, `coord_dependence` (task and coordination families) and
`local_embeddedness` (environmental family); the resource family is the
domain of the rival account (A6).
References: `lawrence1967organization`; `thompson1967organizations`;
`vandeven2013returning`.

**A3. IB applications add two contingencies (T2).** Inside multinationals,
unit structures are fitted to environmental complexity and local resources
(`ghoshal1989internal`, `nohria1994differentiated`), and pressures for
integration and responsiveness differ by business and function
(`prahalad1987multinational`, `venaik2004new`). International settings add
dependence on the host environment and the need for cross-border
coordination, and the two respond to different contingencies
(`meyer2014local`). Framed as applications, not rival theories.

**A4. The IJV as a unit with two parents and a market (interorganizational
extension).** An IJV draws staff from either parent's group or from the
market, at home or abroad. Parental influence in IJVs is differentiated by
activity (`choi2004split`, `mjoen1997control`, `geringer1989control`), and
the two parents differ in their control and its effects (`luo2001dual`,
`chen2009parent`), and where contingency reasoning has reached the IJV, it
explains control, not staff sources: the partners' control systems across
IJVs (`groot2000control`), the fit of control with the parents'
contributions (`barden2005influence`) and with task interdependence
(`han2019management`). Staffing research, by contrast, classifies staff
mainly by nationality (`gong2003subsidiary`, `gaur2007institutional`,
`harzing2001whos`, `inamura2024effect`). Where it adds organizational
origin, it does so for subsidiary general manager successors from inside or
outside the subsidiary (`li2024origin`) or for categories of expatriate
(`georgopoulos2024mediating`), not for the wider workforce of an IJV, and
nationality data alone cannot show whether foreign staff came from a parent
(`inamura2024effect`). The paragraph must distinguish the frame used here
from the two-axis frame of `li2024origin` by unit (IJV), population (all
hires) and the meaning of organizational origin (a parent's group, not the
focal unit).
This paragraph introduces the staffing frame (geographic origin x
organizational origin, plus history not observed) and states that in a
wholly owned subsidiary the two axes largely coincide, whereas a
host-country parent breaks the coincidence.

**A5. Staffing as an integration mechanism.** Hires from a parent carry
routines, knowledge and ties that coordinate interdependent work
(`edstrom1977transfer`, `harzing2001bears`, `harzing2016bridging`);
staffing is one control mechanism among several (`schaan1983parent`,
`chen2009parent`), and decision rights and formal controls are not observed
in the data (`ouchi1979conceptual`, `eisenhardt1985control`).

**A6. Rival account: bargaining power and dependence.** Control follows
resource contributions and bargaining power (`yan2001antecedents`,
`liu2014metaanalysis`, `mjoen1997control`), and dependence shifts as
partners learn (`inkpen1997knowledge`); `pfeffer1978external`.
The account has an explicit IJV precedent: case evidence from Sino-Western
IJVs holds that partners use resource power and expertise to gain decision
power over HRM localization, and that the contingency approach therefore
misses the IJV (`wilson2006dynamics`). The paragraph answers it rather than
leaving it unnamed: power settles who decides at the level of the IJV,
whereas the hypotheses concern differences between domains inside the same
IJV-year.
State what it predicts instead (staffing follows equity and contributions)
and how the design addresses it (IJV x year fixed effects; score x equity
interactions; domain-level check, see A7). Not adopted as a theory pending
the PI's decision.

**A7. Domain-level bargaining.** Name the variant the fixed effects do not
absorb: each parent staffs the activities it contributed (`choi2004split`,
`mohedanosuanes2021control`). Proposed check: parent industry matched to the
occupation group.

## Section B. Hypotheses (contingency level)

### H1. Organizational origin

Statement (brief): the more a function or hierarchical level depends on
coordination with the parents' activities and on parent-specific knowledge,
the larger its share of parent-origin hires relative to independent hires.

Contingency that differs across domains: coordination dependence (task and
coordination families), `coord_dependence` for occupation groups;
`coord_role` for levels.

| Step | Mechanism | References carrying the step |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Domains differ in how interdependent their tasks are with the parents' activities and how much parent-specific knowledge they need | `lawrence1967organization`; `gupta1994organizing`; `han2019management`; `thompson1967organizations` |
| 2 | Interdependent work needs coordination by mutual adjustment and shared routines, which is costly across organizational boundaries | `thompson1967organizations`; `galbraith1973designing`; `galbraith1974organization`; `han2019management` |
| 3 | A hire from a parent's group brings the parent's routines and knowledge and lowers that cost; transferred staff act as coordination and control mechanisms | `edstrom1977transfer`; `harzing2001bears`; `harzing2016bridging`; `delios2000expatriate` |
| 4 | The value of parent-origin staff is highest where the knowledge needed is parent-specific | `fang2010multinational`; `harzing2016bridging`; `lyles1996knowledge` |
| 5 (levels) | Senior levels carry responsibility for coordination with the parents, so the coordination contingency is higher there | `harzing2001whos`; `belderbos2005determinants`; `singh2019subsidiary` (top-position evidence only); `[CITE: hierarchical levels and coordination responsibility]` |

Note: the brief's mechanism also names "personal ties" carried by the hire.
That is an individual-level step and is flagged in `missing-references.md`
(microfoundations flag). The outline keeps the step at the level of the
domain's knowledge and coordination requirement.

Note on levels (claim c, G2 re-run): differences between top managers and
employees in nationality-based localization are documented at the level of
the unit (`inamura2024effect`, `harzing2001whos`). Step 5 is therefore new
only as a comparison of hiring sources across levels inside the same IJV,
not as "localization differs by level".

### H2. Geographic origin

Statement (brief): the more a function depends on host-country institutions,
labor markets, customers and regulators, the larger its share of locally
recruited hires relative to international hires.

Contingency that differs across domains: local embeddedness (environmental
family), `local_embeddedness`.

| Step | Mechanism | References carrying the step |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Domains differ in their dependence on host institutions, customers and regulators | `lawrence1967organization`; `venaik2004new`; `prahalad1987multinational`; `doz1981global` |
| 2 | Host-dependent work needs host-specific knowledge and acceptance by local stakeholders | `kostova2002adoption`; `henisz2000institutional`; `meyer2014local` |
| 3 | Locally recruited staff hold that knowledge and those relationships | `gaur2007institutional` (by contrast; see note); `thompson2004empirical`; `[CITE: host-specific human capital of local recruits]` |

Note: `gaur2007institutional` and `gong2003subsidiary` find that distance
raises parent-country staffing at the unit level. The paragraph must argue
that H2 is a within-IJV, across-domain claim and is compatible with a
unit-level distance effect, which IJV x year fixed effects absorb.

### H3. Channel of localization (anchor)

Statement (brief): among locally recruited hires, the share with prior
employment in a parent's group increases with the domain's coordination
dependence. Boundary: weaker or absent without a host-country parent.

Contingency: the combination of high local embeddedness and coordination
dependence; the two are independent, so a domain can be high on both.

| Step | Mechanism | References carrying the step |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The local and the integration requirements are separate dimensions that respond to different contingencies | `meyer2014local`; `chung2024exploring`; `kostova2002adoption` |
| 2 | A domain high on both needs hires with host-specific and parent-specific knowledge | `georgopoulos2024mediating` (subsidiaries with one parent and a multi-focal strategy favor staff who combine intra-firm and local knowledge, as an analog); `davies2023improving` (host-country nationals with parent experience, as an analog); `henisz2000institutional`; `inkpen1997knowledge` |
| 3 | In an IJV with a host-country parent, the parents' host-country workforce, above all the host-country parent's own, is the largest and cheapest pool of such hires, though not the only one | `inkpen1997knowledge`; `henisz2000institutional`; `luo2001dual`; `georgopoulos2024mediating` (other pools: former inpatriates, self-initiated expatriates); `[CITE: local parent as supplier of host-embedded staff to IJVs]` |
| 4 | Where coordination dependence is low, the open host market offers more candidates at lower cost | `[CITE: recruitment source choice, internal versus external labor markets]` |
| 5 (boundary) | Without a host-country parent the local parent-origin pool is thin, so the slope weakens | `[CITE: IJVs formed by two foreign parents]` |

Note (G2 re-run): step 2 has an analog in units with one parent
(`georgopoulos2024mediating`), so the paragraph must not present the
knowledge combination as a new mechanism. The contribution of H3 rests on
the channel through a second organizational source (step 3) and on the
within-IJV domain slope it implies, which no study within the bounds of the
search has examined (`gap-statement.md`, scope section).

### H4. Dynamic form of H3 (contingency level, no shock)

Statement (brief): when a change in the environment raises the
local-embeddedness requirement of a domain, the share of locally recruited
hires in new hiring rises more in that domain, and the rise consists of
parent-origin hires where coordination dependence is high and of independent
hires where it is low.

Contingency that changes: local embeddedness rises in some domains;
coordination dependence is unchanged.

| Step | Mechanism | References carrying the step |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | An environmental change alters a contingency for some domains and not others | `meyer2022mne`; `witt2019deglobalization`; `doz1981global` |
| 2 | The change creates misfit, and organizations adjust structure to regain fit | `donaldson1987strategy`; `donaldson2001contingency`; `lenguyen2009parent` (control adjustment, indirect) |
| 3 | Adjustment occurs at the margin, in new hiring, and in the domains whose contingency changed | `tang2026strategic`; `adarkwah2024geopolitical`; `[CITE: hiring flows as the adjustment margin]` |
| 4 | Because coordination dependence is unchanged, adjustment runs on the geographic axis while the organizational axis stays where H3 places it | `lawrence1967organization`; `meyer2014local` |
| 5 | Parents' strategic choice limits determinism: the prediction concerns the direction of shifts in shares, not a single fitting configuration | `child1972organizational`; `yan1994bargaining` |

Competing pattern the design must separate: a change that also alters
access to parent staff (mobility) moves the organizational axis directly.

uniform part of the mobility channel; GB-IE IJVs receive the regulatory
change but keep free movement through the Common Travel Area

four years of anticipation, survivor bias (the frame holds only surviving
IJVs; `lei2025playing` shows shocks shift entry away from JVs), single
common onset, and the sparse local parent-origin cell.

## Section D. Positioning paragraph (closing the section)

Restate that the hypotheses predict two axes answering to two
contingencies, that H3's channel exists only in a unit with more than one
organizational source, that the contribution lies in that channel and in the
within-IJV slope rather than in the knowledge-combination step, and that H4
is the dynamic test. References: `lawrence1967organization`,
`meyer2014local`, `luo2001dual`, `meyer2022mne`; `groot2000control` to mark
the step from contingency reasoning on IJV control to IJV staff sources.
