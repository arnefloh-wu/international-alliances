# Memo T3: IJV control and staffing; parent-origin and expatriate staffing (streams S3 and S4)

Stage 2 (lit-synthesis), literature-synthesizer, 2026-11-24.
Evidence basis: worked from abstracts / metadata only. No full texts. Sources
of abstracts as in `memo-t1-structural-contingency.md`. Two corpus records
are not citable: `downes2000knowledge` (unverified) and nguyen2009foreign
(dropped by the PI on 2026-10-04).

Role in the paper (`docs/theory-framework.md`): T3 specifies how integration
and localization are manifested and measured, and supplies the mechanism
evidence for why parent-origin and local hires matter. Since gate 4 the
outcome is the staffing configuration on two axes (geographic origin x
organizational origin, plus history not observed), so this memo reads S3
and S4 for what they say about each axis.

Corpus: S3 has 21 records (6 seeds, 15 includes), S4 has 21 (6 seeds, 15
includes). S3 and S4 are the best-covered streams; one S4 central work
(Belderbos and Heijltjes 2005) was not chased.

## What is established

1. Parental control in IJVs is activity-specific, not only overall. Control
   has a focus (which activities), an extent and mechanisms
   (`geringer1989control`, standard reading, to verify). Ownership share
   alone does not define control; parents control specific activities
   (`mjoen1997control`), and both overall and specific control matter for
   performance from each parent's side (`luo2001dual`). Control is split by
   activity in many IJVs (`beamish1993characteristics`), and split control,
   in which each partner controls the activities that match its
   firm-specific advantages, performs best (`choi2004split`). A normative
   framework assigns dominant, shared or split control per value activity
   from the strategic nature of the activity and relative partner expertise
   (`mohedanosuanes2021control`). Parents control through formal devices,
   staffing and the decision context (`schaan1983parent`). Taken together,
   domain-level differentiation of parental influence is well established for
   control; it has not been shown for hiring sources.

2. Control follows bargaining power and resource contributions. Relative
   bargaining power from the negotiation context and critical resource
   contributions determines control (`yan2001antecedents`; case evidence and
   informal mechanisms in `yan1994bargaining`; conceptual model in
   `yan2001negotiating`). A meta-analysis of 45 studies and 15,252 IJVs
   finds resource contribution, equity share, trust and strategic importance
   as antecedents (`liu2014metaanalysis`). Relative resource input drives
   bargaining power, which shapes equity and activity control
   (`mjoen1997control`). Property-based contributions go with output and
   process control and knowledge-based contributions with social control
   (`chen2009parent`). Dominant-parent JVs are easier to manage
   (`killing1983strategies`, standard reading). This is the rival account.

3. Contingency antecedents of control also exist. Task interdependence
   between partners raises behavior control (`han2019management`); host
   country uncertainty shapes foreign parent control design
   (`lenguyen2008governing`, screening reason only); internal and external
   environments moderate control-performance links (`nguyen2019control`,
   screening reason only); control structure fits resource contribution and
   monitoring ability (`barden2005influence`).

4. The two parents' positions differ. Foreign and local parents differ in
   control practices and in how control relates to their performance
   (`luo2001dual`, `chen2009parent`); control structure relates to conflict
   between parents (`barden2005influence`). The local partner holds the local
   knowledge at the outset, and the foreign partner's acquisition of it
   shifts dependence and destabilizes the venture (`inkpen1997knowledge`).
   A host partner can supply an advantage in dealing with government, though
   it may expropriate (`henisz2000institutional`, T4 record).

5. Staffing from the parent is a coordination and control mechanism.
   Transfers of managers build socialization-based control and informal
   networks that allow decentralization (`edstrom1977transfer`).
   Expatriates control through direct supervision, socialization and
   informal communication networks (`harzing2001bears`). Parent-country
   transfers are used as people-centric control (`patel2019global`), and
   control is the more prominent expatriate role, notably in JVs in China
   (`delios2000expatriate`).

6. Staffing from the parent is also a knowledge-transfer mechanism, and its
   value depends on the knowledge and the function. Expatriate presence in a
   management function raises function-specific knowledge transfer
   (`harzing2016bridging`). Expatriates strengthen the performance effect of
   low-specificity parent knowledge in the short run and weaken it for
   high-specificity knowledge in the long run (`fang2010multinational`).
   The knowledge role is significant in technology- and marketing-intensive
   industries (`delios2000expatriate`); expatriate utilization supports
   subsidiary knowledge creation (`kawai2019expatriate`); successive
   inpatriation builds subsidiary capability (`kim2022how`); IJVs acquire
   knowledge from foreign parents under certain structural conditions
   (`lyles1996knowledge`, standard reading).

7. The geographic mix of staff depends on distance, institutions and time.
   Parent-country nationals are used more in culturally distant
   (`gong2003subsidiary`) and institutionally distant hosts
   (`gaur2007institutional`), with effects that decline as subsidiaries age
   (`gong2003subsidiary`). Societal trust and formal institutions relate to
   subsidiary staffing (`gaur2022societal`, title only). Home-country culture
   and institutions jointly explain ethnocentric top-team staffing
   (`lee2022cultures`); PCN use varies by country of origin, industry and
   subsidiary (`thompson2004empirical`); control and learning motives and
   vertical keiretsu membership explain expatriate managing directors
   (`belderbos2005determinants`); PCN staffing of top positions has a set of
   identified drivers (`harzing2001whos`).

8. Staffing differs by hierarchical level. Most studies measure the top
   position or the top team (`harzing2001whos`, `belderbos2005determinants`,
   `thompson2004empirical`, `lee2022cultures`); `singh2019subsidiary`
   distinguishes PCN presence in the top team from the employee level
   (screening reason only). There is no corpus evidence on middle or
   operational levels in IJVs.

## What is disputed

1. Does host dependence raise or lower local staffing? The brief's H2 says
   host-dependent domains hire locally. The S4 evidence says the opposite at
   the unit level: greater institutional or cultural distance raises the use
   of parent-country staff (`gaur2007institutional`, `gong2003subsidiary`),
   because control and knowledge transfer become harder. The two can be
   reconciled only at the domain level within a unit: distance raises the
   unit's need for parent control overall, while within the unit the
   host-facing domains still need host knowledge. That reconciliation is the
   paper's argument and should be made explicitly; the corpus does not make
   it.

2. Control or knowledge as the reason for parent staffing. `delios2000expatriate`
   finds control more prominent; `harzing2016bridging` and
   `fang2010multinational` emphasize knowledge; `belderbos2005determinants`
   finds both. For the contingency argument both are coordination
   mechanisms, but the bargaining reading treats control staffing as an
   expression of power.

3. Is domain-level parental influence a contingency outcome or a bargaining
   outcome? `choi2004split` and `mohedanosuanes2021control` can be read
   either way: activities are matched to the partner with the relevant
   expertise (a resource contingency at the domain level) or each partner
   controls what it contributed (bargaining at the domain level). This is
   the most important unresolved reading for tension 3, because it varies
   within the IJV and is not absorbed by IJV x year fixed effects.

4. Does localization reduce dependence on the parents? `inkpen1997knowledge`
   implies that local knowledge held by the local partner is a source of
   dependence, while `luo2001dual` shows the local parent's control matters
   for its own performance. Neither separates localization via the local
   parent from localization via the open labor market. H3 sits exactly here.

## What is measured and how

Control: manager surveys with Likert scales of overall and activity-specific
control (`mjoen1997control`, `luo2001dual`, `choi2004split`), control types
(output, process, social; behavior, outcome, clan) in `chen2009parent` and
`han2019management`, typically one informant per IJV and cross-sectional
(`yan2001antecedents`, 90 JVs; `han2019management`, 161 parent firms);
meta-analytic correlations in `liu2014metaanalysis`; case studies
(`yan1994bargaining`, `schaan1983parent`, `mohedanosuanes2021control`); a
dynamic survey over the life cycle in `lenguyen2009parent` (49 IJVs).
Staffing: nationality-based ratios from archival databases of Japanese
subsidiaries (`gong2003subsidiary`; `gaur2007institutional`, 12,997
subsidiaries; `fang2010multinational`, 1,660 subsidiaries;
`belderbos2005determinants`, expatriate managing director dummy); survey
counts of expatriates per management function (`harzing2016bridging`);
top-position nationality (`harzing2001whos`, `thompson2004empirical`,
`lee2022cultures`). No corpus study classifies staff by prior employer, and
none observes the parent organization of local staff.

## What is missing for this project

1. An organizational-origin dimension. Every staffing study in the corpus
   classifies by nationality (PCN, HCN, third-country national). None
   separates a host-country employee who comes from the local parent from
   one hired on the open market (bounded to the provisional corpus).
2. IJV staffing below the top position and by function. Function-level
   staffing evidence exists only for expatriates in subsidiaries
   (`harzing2016bridging`).
3. IJVs formed by two foreign parents, which the brief uses as the boundary
   condition of H3 `[CITE: studies of IJVs with no host-country parent]`.
4. Evidence on whether staffing differentiation tracks contingencies after
   the IJV-level bargaining outcome is held constant. The corpus measures
   control and bargaining at the IJV level only.
5. A labor-market account of when a unit hires from the market rather than
   an affiliated organization (supply, cost, search) for H3's low-coordination
   branch `[CITE: recruitment source choice, internal versus external labor
   markets]`.
6. Evidence on seconded staff who keep the parent as employer of record and
   are therefore invisible in profile data `[CITE: secondment and
   employer-of-record in IJVs]`.

## Resource dependence: evidence for the two readings (for the PI)

Rival reading (dependence on parent resources is the resource contingency;
bargaining power and resource dependence are the rival account). Supported
by: tension 3 in `docs/theory-framework.md`, which requires contingencies and
parent power to stay separable; the corpus measures bargaining at the IJV
level (`yan2001antecedents`, `liu2014metaanalysis`, `mjoen1997control`),
which the within-IJV design absorbs; the predictions only resource
dependence adds (which parent staffs which function; drift toward
independent hiring with age) rest on the weakest data (brief, Appendix C).

Complement reading (adopt resource dependence theory alongside contingency
theory). Supported by: the consistency of the resource-contribution finding
(`liu2014metaanalysis`, `chen2009parent`, `yan2001antecedents`); the
dependence framing of IJV instability (`inkpen1997knowledge`); the explicit
use of resource dependence in recent corpus work (`plabarber2021intermediate`,
`lei2025playing`); and above all the activity-level matching of control to
partner expertise (`choi2004split`, `mohedanosuanes2021control`), which is
a within-IJV pattern the fixed effects do not absorb and which a pure
contingency account would have to call a "resource contingency" anyway.

Synthesizer's reading of the evidence, for the PI to accept or reject: the
corpus favors the rival treatment for the IJV-level component of bargaining
and leaves the domain-level component unresolved. If the rival treatment is
kept, the outline needs an explicit test of the domain-level variant (for
example, whether a parent's industry matches the occupation group) and the
original theory source `[CITE: Pfeffer & Salancik 1978]`, which is not in the
bibliography.

## Implication for our theory section

T3 carries the mechanism steps of H1 (parent-origin staff as coordination
and knowledge carriers: `edstrom1977transfer`, `harzing2001bears`,
`harzing2016bridging`) and the definition of the staffing frame, and it
locates the contribution: control is known to be differentiated by activity
in IJVs (`choi2004split`, `mjoen1997control`, `geringer1989control`), but
staffing research has only a geographic (nationality) axis. The theory
section has to confront the S4 finding that distance raises parent staffing
(`gaur2007institutional`, `gong2003subsidiary`) by arguing at the domain
level within the IJV. The bargaining literature
(`yan2001antecedents`, `liu2014metaanalysis`, `choi2004split`) enters as the
rival account, with the domain-level variant named as the one the design
must address separately.
