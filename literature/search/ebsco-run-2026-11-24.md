## Does anything threaten the novelty of H3 or of the two-parent contingency claim?

A deeper read of these records, with full texts where they are open, is
being written by a separate agent in
`literature/search/ebsco-novelty-check-2026-11-24.md`. That file did not
exist when this memo was written. The answer below rests on the abstracts
in the exports only. Where the novelty check differs, its conclusions take
precedence.

No record in the three exports tests H3 or something close to it. None
observes the prior employers of an IJV's staff, none separates local hires
from a parent's host-country workforce and local hires from the open
market, and none relates the hiring source to the coordination dependence of
a function. None tests contingency fit in a unit with two parents whose
requirements differ.

Four records come nearest and should be read in full first:

1. EB-S7-09, Wilson, Chen & Erakovic (2006). Two parents push different HRM
   practices into the IJV's localization process, and the authors say the
   contingency approach misses this partner-power dynamic. This does not
   threaten novelty, but it is a published objection to the paper's framing:
   localization in IJVs is driven by parent decision power, not by
   contingencies. It bears directly on tension 3 of
   `docs/theory-framework.md` (contingencies against bargaining power).
2. EB-S7-22, Georgopoulos et al. (2024). This is the nearest analogue to the
   mechanism of H3: staff who combine intra-firm and local knowledge (former
   inpatriates) are used where a subsidiary needs both integration and
   responsiveness. It concerns wholly owned subsidiaries at the strategy
   level and uses nationality categories, so it leaves the IJV, function and
   organizational-origin claim open. The paper will have to cite it and say
   how H3 differs.
3. EB-S7-02, Makhija & Ganesh (1997). A conceptual treatment of two partners
   with different requirements setting the controls of a JV, which matters
   for the two-parent contingency claim.
4. EB-S3-04, Li & Schotter (2020, proceedings). IJV staffing with the
   foreign parent's nationals in local-partner-dominated IJVs, at the general
   manager level.

EB-S3-01 (in Japanese) is fifth if the PI or a co-author can read it or
waives the language rule.

How much weight this null result carries. It comes from one database and 152
records. S3-local, the block most directly aimed at H3, returned only 5
records, because all three concept groups are restricted to the abstract
and the local-partner group admits four phrases only. S7-multiparent
overlaps strongly with the OpenAlex queries of the same day (6 of 15 records
were already in the sheet), which suggests the earlier search had covered
much of that block. The answer is therefore "nothing found in EBSCO", not
"nothing exists". A Web of Science run of S3-local, which is broader, would
be the cheapest further check.

## Noise patterns and suggested tightening for a later Web of Science run

S7-staffing (132 records, 4 included).

- `misfit` without qualification is the main source of noise. In 62 of the
  132 records the contingency group is met only by "misfit" (no "contingency
  theory", "contingency approach", "contingency perspective" or "structural
  contingency" in title or abstract). Of the 60 unique ones, 59 are excluded
  and one is a `scope:` record. They are person-organization, person-job and
  person-environment fit studies (20 records name such a fit) and colloquial
  uses ("misfits") in trade press.
- "Contingency approach" and "contingency perspective" also retrieve micro
  HRM, leadership (Fiedler), marketing and health-services studies.
- EBSCO's equivalent-subject expansion widened the staffing group. 44
  records have none of the string's staffing terms in title, abstract or
  keywords and carry subject headings such as "Employees" or "Employee
  selection". Two of the eleven included or unsure records came in this way
  (EB-S7-23, EB-S7-114), so the expander helped recall a little and added
  much noise.
- The limiters were not applied: 22 periodical, newspaper or trade items,
  27 conference proceedings and 5 records from before 1985.

Suggested Web of Science string for S7-staffing. It requires an
organizational unit, qualifies `misfit`, drops bare `recruit*` and `hiring`,
removes person-level fit and keeps the category and document-type filters
of the protocol. Test it on the first 100 records before exporting. If
Web of Science rejects the proximity term, run `misfit` only inside the
unit group.

```
TS=(("contingency theory" OR "structural contingency" OR "contingency approach" OR "contingency perspective" OR "structural fit" OR (misfit NEAR/3 (structur* OR strateg* OR environment*))) AND (staffing OR "workforce composition" OR "personnel composition" OR "employee composition" OR expatriat* OR "parent-country national*" OR "host-country national*" OR "internal hiring" OR "external hiring" OR "recruitment source*") AND (subsidiar* OR "joint venture*" OR multinational* OR "business unit*" OR "foreign-owned" OR alliance*) NOT ("person-organi?ation fit" OR "person-environment fit" OR "person-job fit" OR "job seeker*"))
```

The Web of Science string in `search-strings.md` also contains
`"organi?ational fit"`, which in Web of Science will retrieve the
person-organization fit literature at scale. The version above leaves it out.

S7-multiparent (15 records, 4 included and 1 unsure). The noise is modest:
JV formation and entry-mode studies (2), a public-private housing
partnership and a teaching case. The bare term `"information processing"`
and the default-field group `structure* OR design OR control` let these in.
For Web of Science, replace `"information processing"` with
`"information processing requirement*" OR "information-processing view"`,
and add a variant aimed at the two-parent question:

```
TS=(("joint venture*" OR IJV*) AND ("parent* conflict*" OR "conflicting demand*" OR "competing demand*" OR "divergent goal*" OR "dual control" OR "multiple principal*" OR "conflicting requirement*") AND (structur* OR staffing OR control OR design OR "human resource*"))
```

S3-local (5 records, 2 included). The problem is recall, not noise. The
Web of Science string in `search-strings.md` is already broader (it adds
host-country partners, parent firms and companies, secondment and transferred
employees). Its likely noise comes from `"parent firm*"` and
`"parent compan*"`, which retrieve formation and performance studies. To
keep recall and limit that noise, tie the partner terms to staff terms:

```
TS=(("joint venture*" OR IJV*) AND (("local partner*" OR "local parent*" OR "host partner*" OR "host-country partner*" OR "domestic partner*" OR "parent firm*" OR "parent compan*") NEAR/10 (employee* OR staff* OR manager* OR personnel OR seconded OR secondment OR transfer* OR recruit*)))
```

These are suggestions for the PI. They are not entered in
`search-strings.md` and have not been run.
