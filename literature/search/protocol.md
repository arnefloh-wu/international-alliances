## Aim

Identify the literature needed to (a) establish structural contingency
theory as the main theory and show how it has been applied in
international business, (b) position the construct of differentiated
international integration against existing alliance- and MNE-level
integration concepts, (c) derive the mechanisms behind the hypotheses
from contingency logic, (d) justify the measurement of integration
through employees' prior organizational affiliation, and (e) identify
and compare candidate geopolitical shocks as sources of changing
contingencies. The hierarchy of tiers is defined in
`docs/theory-framework.md`.

Note added in v1.2: this aim was written against `initial-research-idea.md`,
whose hypotheses H1 to H5 take cross-partner integration (staff drawn from
the parent firms) as the outcome, and point (d) refers to that measure.
Since gate 4 (2026-11-24) the hypotheses are H1 to H4 of
`docs/research-brief.md`, stated on the staffing of functions and levels by
geographic and organizational origin. "The hypotheses" in (c) and the
measurement in (d) are read in that sense; gate 5 fixes the final set. The
text above is kept as written. Point (f) is added in v1.2: (f) identify
occupational task data that can measure the contingencies of functions and
occupations before outcomes are seen (stream S10).

## Inclusion criteria (title and abstract stage)

Include if the paper (a) theorizes or measures integration, coordination,
control, autonomy, staffing or localization at the level of a unit,
function, team, hierarchical level or individual inside an MNE, IJV or
alliance; or (b) studies IJV organization, control or staffing; or (c)
studies how geopolitical or regulatory change alters the organization or
staffing of foreign operations or alliances; or (d) uses employment-
history data to measure organizational composition.

Exclude if the paper (a) treats the alliance or subsidiary only as a
whole with no internal differentiation and does not concern IJV control
or staffing; (b) is about alliance formation, partner selection or
performance outcomes only; (c) is a non-scholarly source; (d) is not in
English or German.

Amendments in v1.1:

- Include also if the paper (e) develops, tests or reviews structural
  contingency theory, contingency fit or misfit, or structural
  adjustment to environmental change; (f) concerns organizational design
  across functions or hierarchical levels, decision rights or control
  mechanisms; or (g) studies the effects of sanctions, investment
  screening, export controls, mobility or visa restrictions, or bilateral
  political deterioration on firms' organization, staffing or alliances
  (stream S9; also used for candidate-shock evidence).
- Do not include a paper only because it offers individual-level
  microfoundations (for example identity, trust or social-tie mechanisms)
  unless it also meets (a) to (g). Microfoundations are outside the
  framework unless the interviews call for them (`docs/theory-framework.md`).

Amendments in v1.2 (gate 2, 2026-11-24, decisions 8 and 9):

- Include also if the paper (h) develops, validates or applies
  occupational task descriptors (O\*NET, DOT, PIAAC or comparable national
  or European task indices) as measures of the task environment,
  contingencies, task interdependence, coordination requirements, or
  external, customer or face-to-face contact, at the level of the
  occupation, job, function or organization; or assesses whether such
  descriptors transfer to countries other than the US (stream S10).
  Exclude from S10 a paper that (i) uses task data only as a control or as
  input to a labor-market outcome (wages, inequality, automation or AI
  exposure, health) without building or testing a measure of the kind named
  in (h); (ii) is a job analysis or competency model for a single occupation
  or profession; or (iii) uses "task" in a sense unrelated to occupational
  descriptors (for example team task design or work-family roles).
  Exclusion criteria (a) and (b) do not apply to S10, which is a
  measurement stream like S6; (c) and (d) apply.
- Function-level records for S2: a paper that compares integration,
  responsiveness or localization across business functions or value-chain
  activities meets criterion (a) even when the unit studied is the whole
  subsidiary or firm, provided the comparison is made by function or
  activity.
- Mechanism-support records. A record outside criteria (a) to (g), and
  not covered by (h), that concerns internal versus external hiring
  (internal or group labor markets, intra-group mobility, or single-firm
  HRM evidence on the hiring source) is admitted only as support for the
  hiring-source step of H3 in `docs/research-brief.md`, that is, whether a
  domain draws staff from a parent's workforce or from the open labor
  market. Its screening reason starts with `scope:`, it is used for no other
  claim, and it is never the sole support for a hypothesis. In the
  2026-11-24 follow-up, 27 records carry a `scope:` reason. Nine (`OA-S4-*`)
  are hiring-source records under this rule. Seventeen (`OA-S6-*`) are
  O\*NET and task-data records that were filed under S6 for lack of a task
  stream; they may be re-tagged S10 in `screening.csv` and are then screened
  against (h) instead of this rule. One (`OA-S7-63`, contingency theory with
  staffing as the outcome of a regulatory change) is screened against (e)
  and the publication-type rule below. Re-tagging is done in the screening
  sheet, not in this protocol.
- Publication types. Conference proceedings (including Academy of
  Management Proceedings abstracts, citable since the decision of
  2026-10-04), working papers and preprints (for example NBER, SSRN,
  RePEc, OECD or World Bank papers) are not non-scholarly sources under
  exclusion criterion (c). They are admitted when no peer-reviewed version
  exists; the searcher checks Crossref and Google Scholar for a later
  journal or book version, and if one exists the peer-reviewed version is
  screened and the earlier record is excluded as `F_duplicate`. Admitted
  records are flagged in `literature/references.bib` as having no
  peer-reviewed version (the flag is set by the references stage) and are
  never the sole support for a hypothesis. Protocol v1.1 did not cover
  dissertations; from v1.2 they follow the same rule. The seed Schaan 1983,
  identified at Stage 1 as a dissertation, stays a seed, and the flag and
  the sole-support rule apply to it. This answers the open item of
  2026-10-02 on proceedings, working papers and dissertations
  (`docs/decisions.md`).

## Screening

Two-stage: title/abstract, then full text for items marked include or
unsure. Every exclusion has a reason code: `A_level` (alliance-level
only), `B_formation` (formation/performance only), `C_nonscholarly`,
`D_language`, `E_offtopic`, `F_duplicate`.

v1.2: the codes are unchanged. S10 exclusions use `E_offtopic` with a
phrase naming the S10 exclusion ground. Mechanism-support records carry a
reason that starts with `scope:` whatever their decision. Records admitted
under the publication-type rule name their type in the reason or notes
(proceedings, working paper, preprint, dissertation).

## Citation chase

Backward and forward citation search on the five most-cited included
works per stream. The Consensus free tier cannot support this (no
cited-by lists, 3 searches left until 2026-11-01); Google Scholar
"cited by" run manually is the primary route. Forward citations via Consensus or Google Scholar
"cited by", logged with date.
