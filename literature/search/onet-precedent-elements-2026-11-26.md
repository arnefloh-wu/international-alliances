## 2. Dierdorff & Ellington (2008)

Method section "Measures", p. 886 (PDF page 5); descriptive statistics and alphas in Table 2, p. 888 (PDF page 7); linking procedure in "Procedure", p. 886.

- Data: archival O\*NET occupation-level data linked to 1,367 respondents of the 2002 General Social Survey "Quality of Working Life" module in 126 occupations. The authors matched the respondents' Census occupation codes and titles to specific O\*NET occupations by hand and dropped cases with no clear match (p. 886). The O\*NET release is not named.
- Scale: the O\*NET values are "standardized to range from 1 to 100 to facilitate cross-occupational comparisons" (p. 886). The interdependence items are described as "rated on a 5-point importance scale" (p. 886); in the O\*NET database these are work context items on the CX scale (1 to 5; the database also carries the CXP category percentages).
- Interdependence (Hypothesis 1): three items, quoted in the paper as interactions that require the respondent to "work with or contribute to a work group or team?", to "deal with external customers (as in retail sales) or the public in general (as in police work)?", and to "coordinate or lead others in accomplishing work activities (not as a supervisor or team leader)?" (p. 886). The paper gives no element IDs. The wording matches the O\*NET 31.0 descriptions of 4.C.1.b.1.e (Work With or Contribute to a Work Group or Team), 4.C.1.b.1.f (Deal With External Customers or the Public in General) and 4.C.1.b.1.g (Coordinate or Lead Others in Accomplishing Work Activities) word for word, so the mapping is not in doubt.
- Responsibility for others: two items on a 5-point responsibility scale, work outcomes and results of other workers and health and safety of other workers (pp. 886-887), which match 4.C.1.c.2 and 4.C.1.c.1.
- Interpersonal conflict: three items on a 5-point frequency scale, conflict situations, unpleasant, angry or discourteous people, and violent or physically aggressive people (p. 887), which match 4.C.1.d.1, 4.C.1.d.2 and 4.C.1.d.3.
- Aggregation: occupation level only (Level 2 of a hierarchical linear model). Coefficient alpha .86 for interdependence and .87 for interpersonal conflict across the 126 occupations (Table 2, p. 888); the interdependence mean is 61.89 (SD 22.01) on the 1 to 100 metric. The text does not say whether the items were averaged or summed. A confirmatory factor analysis supports three distinct factors (p. 887).

## 3. Oldenski (2012), working paper of October 2011

Section 4, "Measures of Communication and Complexity", pp. 9-11; Table 2, p. 27.

- Concepts: communication with customers ("the transmission of information between firms and consumers") and complex within-firm communication, captured through the Autor, Levy and Murnane routine and nonroutine distinction: nonroutine tasks "can not ... be fully described in words when communicated from a headquarters firm to its affiliate" (p. 9). Within-firm communication is therefore measured by task complexity, not by an interaction or coordination element.
- Measures (p. 11): "working with the public" as the proxy for communicating with consumers; "creative thinking" for task complexity; "making decisions and solving problems" and "communicating inside the organization" as alternative non-routine measures (robustness); "handling objects", "operating machines (other than vehicles)" and "general physical activities" for routine manual tasks. A principal-components robustness check combines creativity, problem solving, giving consultation or advice, developing objectives, communicating internally and working with computers into one non-routine component; no component is built for customer communication "because working directly with the public is the single O\*NET task that corresponds directly to that concept" (p. 11). Table 2 (p. 27) reports five tasks: communicating with customers, creative thinking, problem solving/decisions, handling objects, operating machines.
- Element IDs: none given. Nearest O\*NET 31.0 elements by title (agent's mapping, not stated by the author): working with the public, 4.A.4.a.8 Performing for or Working Directly with the Public; creative thinking, 4.A.2.b.2 Thinking Creatively; making decisions and solving problems, 4.A.2.b.1; communicating inside the organization, probably 4.A.4.a.2 Communicating with Supervisors, Peers, or Subordinates; giving consultation or advice, 4.A.4.b.6; developing objectives, 4.A.2.b.4; working with computers, 4.A.3.b.1; handling objects, 4.A.3.a.2; operating machines other than vehicles, 4.A.3.a.3 Controlling Machines and Processes; general physical activities, 4.A.3.a.1. The mapping of "working with the public" to the generalized work activity 4.A.4.a.8 rather than the work context item 4.C.1.b.1.f is supported by the scale (below): work context items carry no importance scale. It is still an inference.
- Scale: the O\*NET 0 to 100 importance score of each task in each occupation (footnote 5, p. 10), that is, the importance (IM) scale rescaled.
- Aggregation (p. 10, equations 6 and 7): occupation scores are aggregated to BEA industries (versions of 3-digit SIC) with weights equal to each occupation's share of industry employment from the BLS Occupational Employment Statistics, M_sz = sum over c of theta_zc s_c; each industry score is then divided by the sum over the tasks used, I_sz = M_sz / sum over s of M_sz, giving relative task intensities. Raw M_sz gives qualitatively similar results (footnote 6, p. 11). The O\*NET release is not named.

Overlap: Dierdorff & Ellington use exactly the plan's three work context items (4.C.1.b.1.e, f, g), but combine them into a single interdependence score with alpha .86, whereas the plan puts 4.C.1.b.1.f into `local_embeddedness` and the other two into `coord_dependence`. Oldenski uses none of the plan's six narrow elements; her customer-contact measure corresponds most closely to 4.A.4.a.8, which is in the plan's broad local-embeddedness set, and her within-firm communication construct is task complexity (creative thinking), not coordination. Neither study uses law and government knowledge, communicating with people outside the organization, or coordinating the work and activities of others.

Implication for the plan (proposal, not an edit): the sentence at lines 658 to 662 can say that Dierdorff & Ellington used the same three work context items as one interdependence measure and that Oldenski used O\*NET importance ratings of working with the public and of creative thinking as customer-contact and complexity measures. Because Dierdorff & Ellington treat dealing with external customers as part of interdependence, the methods section should justify assigning 4.C.1.b.1.f to local embeddedness (for example by the correlation pattern reported in `data/interim/contingency-scores-v1-2026-11-25.md`), and a robustness check without 4.C.1.b.1.f in either score would answer the obvious objection.

## 5. Suggested wording for the methods section

Occupation-level O\*NET ratings of job interactions have been used as work characteristics before: @dierdorff2008its combined the work context items on working in a team, dealing with external customers and coordinating or leading others into one interdependence score, and @oldenski2012export weighted the importance of working with the public and of creative thinking by industry employment shares to measure customer contact and complex within-firm communication. We use the same three work context items but assign dealing with external customers to local embeddedness and the other two to coordination dependence, and we add knowledge of law and government, communicating with people outside the organization, and coordinating the work and activities of others.
