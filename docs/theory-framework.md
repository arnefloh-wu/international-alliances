# Theoretical framework and literature hierarchy

Decided by the PI on 2026-10-02 (see `docs/decisions.md`). Every agent that
reads literature, codes interviews, specifies hypotheses or writes theory
text follows this hierarchy. The research idea (`initial-research-idea.md`)
names the differentiated-network perspective as its base; this document
supersedes that framing. The research idea itself is left unchanged until
the PI revises it.

## The hierarchy

| Tier | Role in the paper | Content | Streams |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 Main theory | Carries the core argument and all hypotheses | Structural contingency theory: organizational domains face different task, environmental, resource and coordination contingencies, so no single optimal degree or form of integration exists for the whole alliance; functions and hierarchical levels need different arrangements; environmental change alters the contingencies and triggers selective reconfiguration. | S7 |
| T2 Related IB frameworks | Show how contingency logic has been applied in international business; used to position and define terms | The differentiated network (Nohria and Ghoshal) and the integration-responsiveness framework (Prahalad and Doz). Treated as IB applications of the same broader contingency logic, not as separate theories. | S1, S2 |
| T3 Supporting literatures | Specify how integration is manifested and measured | IJV control and staffing; parent-origin and expatriate staffing; organizational-design work where it clarifies differentiation across functions, levels, decision rights and control mechanisms. | S3, S4, S8 |
| T4 Environmental context | Source of changing contingencies | Geopolitics and the candidate shocks (sanctions, investment screening, export controls, mobility restrictions, bilateral political deterioration). Context, not theory. | S5, S9 |
| Methods | Justify the archival measurement | Employment-history data in management research. | S6 |
| Excluded | | Microfoundations. Added only if the interview analysis shows a clear individual-level mechanism that the contingency logic cannot explain and that the paper needs. | none |

Stream IDs S1 to S6 were already run on 2026-10-02 and keep their numbers.
S7 to S9 are new in protocol version 1.1.

## How the tiers enter the manuscript

1. The theory section is built on T1. Each hypothesis states the contingency
   that differs across domains or changes with the environment, then the
   expected arrangement.
2. T2 appears as "IB applications of contingency logic". The text must not
   present the differentiated network and integration-responsiveness as
   rival or additional theories, and must not use them as the anchor for a
   hypothesis. Their role is to show that international settings add
   contingencies (host-country embeddedness, cross-border coordination)
   and to supply established vocabulary.
3. T3 appears where the construct is defined and measured, and in the
   measurement rationale: why prior organizational affiliation indicates
   cross-partner integration, parent dominance and localization, and why
   decision rights and control mechanisms are not directly observable in
   the archival data.
4. T4 appears in the dynamic hypotheses and the shock choice, framed as a
   change in contingencies.

## Contingency dimensions and the measures

The research idea scores each function on two summary dimensions
(`coord_dependence`, `local_embeddedness`). Under structural contingency
theory these are summaries of four contingency families. The qualitative
phase decides whether two summary scores suffice or whether a third
(dependence on parent resources) is needed.

| Contingency family | Examples | Maps to |
|---|---|---|
| Task | interdependence among partners' activities, task uncertainty, knowledge intensity | `coord_dependence` (H1) |
| Coordination | need for joint decisions, decision-right allocation, hierarchical responsibility for alliance-wide coordination | `coord_dependence`, `coord_role` (H3, H5) |
| Environmental | dependence on host institutions, labor markets, customers, regulators; political sensitivity | `local_embeddedness` (H2, H4) |
| Resource | dependence on parent technology, personnel, finance; access to parent resources | candidate third score, set at gate 3 |

A shock is theoretically useful to the extent that it changes one or more
of these contingency families by different amounts in different domains.

## Tensions the synthesis must address

Agents must not hide these. The synthesizer's gap statement and the
internal reviewer's report both address them.

1. Classical contingency theory is static and sometimes read as
   deterministic. The paper's dynamic claim (selective reconfiguration
   after a contingency change) needs the literature on adjustment to
   regain fit and on multiple or misaligned contingencies, and must say how
   strategic choice by the parents limits determinism.
2. Contingency theory was developed for single organizations. An IJV has
   two parents whose requirements may conflict. This interorganizational
   twist is where the IB-specific contribution has to come from, because a
   JIBS editor will ask what the paper adds beyond applying the theory to
   a new setting.
3. Observable staffing is an outcome of contingencies and of parent
   bargaining power. Ownership asymmetry and parent dominance must stay
   separable in both theory and measurement.

## Rules for agents

- Do not reintroduce the differentiated network as the main theory in any
  outline, memo or draft.
- Do not add microfoundations. Interview analysts record any candidate
  individual-level mechanism in the dedicated section of the measurement
  memo; the PI decides.
- Hypotheses are written at the contingency level first and instantiated
  for the chosen shock second (see the pre-analysis plan procedure in
  `docs/workflow.md`).
- Seed references for each tier are listed in
  `literature/search/protocol.md`. They are from memory and unverified until
  the reference-manager confirms them.
